Three ways to say it, in the order a user would meet them — a label that is always there, a prompt at the moment it matters, and chrome that confirms the mode while it is armed. Flip Space held to see which of them respond to the key.

The only one that reports state rather than giving instructions. The chip is dim at rest and lights up the moment Space goes down, in the same accent-on-soft treatment the armed tool button already uses; the cursor turns to a grab hand at the same instant. A user who has pressed Space once never wonders again whether it took — but on its own it teaches nobody, because nothing invites the first press.
Shipped — B with C
B does the teaching, once, at the only moment the lesson is useful; C does the confirming, forever, for the cost of one 90-px chip that is dim until it is relevant. Between them nothing has to stand in the sub bar shouting an instruction at people who already know it, which is why A was left out.
